# Brief and feedback

The quality bar for a launch video, and the corrections that shaped this skill.
Treat each correction as a failure mode to avoid on the first pass.

## Brief

> Make a dynamic ~30-second motion graphics video that shows what an incredible
> motion designer you are, like it's your showreel for a résumé. Go all out.
> Introduce the project: its purpose, visuals of how it works behind the scenes
> (the audience is technical), the models or engines it relies on and why they
> matter, and its headline measured result. It must have music, and the motion
> must match the music. Make it a real professional production, not a demo or
> prototype. Favor more animation and motion design; never use screenshots raw.
> Break them down into components and icons so those animate too. Make the
> motion juicy, using the best motion design techniques available.

## Feedback, in order

1. **Preview showed a near-empty frame.** The social upload preview showed the
   cold open's first sparse frames. Tell: the thumbnail is blank or nearly blank.
2. **A single poster frame at frame 0 was not enough.** The feed still showed a
   frame a few frames in. Tell: a one-frame poster is skipped; the thumbnail
   needs a hold.
3. **Dark poster before a light opening.** Keep the pre-roll on the opening's
   background so playback does not start with a flash.
4. **The product in action beats a stats slide as the preview.** The preferred
   thumbnail was the reveal frame (logo, tagline, terminal with a finished
   command and its output), not the benchmark key art. The key art stays useful
   as a README image.
5. **Keep the source.** The render project belongs in the repo, not a scratch
   directory.

## What landed well

A problem cold open, a beat drop into the logo reveal, a dark behind-the-scenes
section built from animated icons, a slam on the headline number with its
disclosure intact, and a lockup with the install command.
